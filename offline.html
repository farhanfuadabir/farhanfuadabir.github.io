Away from the keyboard

Papers are a narrow view of a person. This is the rest of it: the soldering iron, the students, the community, and a long streak of competitions I mostly did not win.

Tinkering

I came into machine learning through hardware, and I never really left. Before I was training models I was etching boards, and that has stuck. Most of what I enjoy has a physical output: a robot that follows a line, a glove that reads a gesture, a buzzer that decides who was first.

The habit is useful. Working with sensors teaches you that data is something a physical thing produced under specific conditions, not a matrix that fell out of the sky. That instinct shows up in how I think about ultrasound, where the probe angle and the operator's hand are part of the dataset whether you model them or not.

Robots

PCBs

Wearables
Flex sensors, IMUs, and the long tail of problems that come with putting electronics on a moving human.
